02 - Vittorio Emanuele II Technical Institute.

In memory of Ernesto and Ada Rossi

Historical context

We are standing in front of the historic headquarters of the Vittorio Emanuele II Technical Institute. During the Fascist twenty-year period, this building was not only a place of education, but also a center of surveillance and control. The most radical transformation occurred during the Nazi occupation (1943–1945): the building was requisitioned by the Germans and turned into a strategic military headquarters. Due to the occupation, educational activities were interrupted and the school was forced to relocate elsewhere, leaving these classrooms in the hands of military command until Liberation.

Why it is a place of memory

Ernesto Rossi transformed his chair of economics at the Vittorio Emanuele Institute into a political trench. A leader of the Giustizia e Libertà movement, he used Bergamo as a strategic hub to connect internal resistance with resistance in exile. He paid for this courage with his arrest in 1930 and thirteen years of imprisonment, during which he contributed to writing the Ventotene Manifesto for a united Europe. Ada Rossi, a scientist and a profoundly independent woman, was the backbone of civil resistance in the city. Dismissed by the regime, she never stopped fighting: through her private lessons, she shaped the democratic conscience of dozens of young people from Bergamo, laying the foundations for the future local Resistance. It was she, acting as a silent courier, who carried texts out of confinement that would go on to define European identity. Together, Ernesto and Ada demonstrated that freedom is defended both through political action and through the education of young people.

The more than 900 letters exchanged during imprisonment were not merely words, but instruments of struggle. Ada secretly read them to her students to educate them in freedom: “In Bergamo they are read by thirty people and they remain astonished by your courage.” In those writings, Ernesto reaffirmed that “no defeat is final as long as one refuses to accept it,” transforming the pain of prison into a civic consciousness for the entire city.

In depth: The Ventotene Manifesto

The Ventotene Manifesto is a political document written in 1941 by Ernesto Rossi and Altiero Spinelli, with the contribution of Eugenio Colorni, during their confinement on the island of Ventotene, in the midst of the Second World War. It represents one of the foundational texts of European federalist thought. The authors identified nationalism and the absolute sovereignty of states as the main cause of the wars that devastated Europe. For this reason, they proposed overcoming the traditional nation-state and building a European Federation, equipped with common institutions—such as a government and a parliament—with real decision-making powers. The Manifesto also establishes the need for deep economic integration, based on a single market, shared resources, and common policies in production and social affairs, aimed at reducing inequalities and ensuring dignified living conditions. Particular attention is given to social justice, the protection of workers, and the promotion of rights. The Ventotene Manifesto influenced the process of building a united Europe, inspiring the creation of the first Community institutions, such as the ECSC and the European Economic Community. The ultimate goal of the project is the achievement of stable and lasting peace: a politically and economically united Europe would make new conflicts between European peoples impossible.
