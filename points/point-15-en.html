15 – Montelungo Barracks

Montelungo Barracks

Historical context

Occupied by German forces after their arrival in Bergamo, the barracks formerly belonging to the 78th Infantry Regiment (stationed here since 1921) were identified as a possible transit camp. Although Fossoli was later chosen instead, between early March and April 1944 the site was used as a transit camp for 850 prisoners (including 43 women) to be deported to Germany. Most were arrested during the March 1944 strikes. Two transports left Bergamo railway station on 17 March and 5 April, heading to Mauthausen, while the women were later transferred to Auschwitz-Birkenau.

During their detention in Bergamo, prisoners were held in the first-floor dormitories overlooking Vicolo San Giovanni. Relatives, especially women, gathered there hoping to contact them. From the windows, prisoners threw down notes and farewell messages, often collected and sent to families by passers-by.

On 17 March, prisoners were forced to march on foot to the railway station: some citizens offered support, others mocked them. On 5 April, German authorities transported detainees by bus to avoid public unrest.

Why this is a place of memory

Montelungo Barracks is an important site of memory because it embodies the mechanism of political repression and the transition from civil resistance to deportation. It symbolically connects collective actions such as the March 1944 strikes to the arrest system and subsequent deportation to concentration camps.

Many of the prisoners later passed through the Grumellina transit camp. Remembering this place means acknowledging how institutional violence often originated in ordinary urban buildings. Today, it reminds us of the cost of freedom and the courage of those who maintained their dignity even in the face of deportation.

Grumellina Transit Camp

During World War II, the Grumellina area (Grumello al Piano) hosted POW Camp No. 62 for prisoners of war and interned soldiers.

Thousands of men of different nationalities were held there in harsh conditions. Today it is a site of memory preserving their suffering and stories of solidarity.

"Capture is marked above all by surprise. For some there is almost blissful unconsciousness... the failure of these hopes causes neither anger nor disappointment, but seems like an inevitable fate."
From Streikertransport; Political Deportation in the Industrial Area of Sesto San Giovanni (Giuseppe Valota, 2007)
