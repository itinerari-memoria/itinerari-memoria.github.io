Point 03 - State Scientific High School "Filippo Lussana"

Itineraries of Memory 2.0

Historical context

The “Filippo Lussana” Scientific High School in Bergamo fits into a historical context deeply marked by the Fascist regime’s educational policy. From the early 1920s, control of schools became one of the main tools through which Fascism sought to shape new generations. The 1923 school reform, developed by Giovanni Gentile, established a strongly selective and hierarchical system: classical and humanistic training was considered the only path able to forge the future ruling class, while technical and vocational education was relegated to a subordinate role.

Born in Castelvetrano (Sicily) on 30 May 1875, he was an Italian philosopher, educator, and politician, known as the “philosopher of Fascism.” He served as Minister of Public Instruction between 1922 and 1924, authoring the school reform that bears his name (1923). As the theorist of the “ethical state,” he provided the theoretical foundations for Mussolini’s regime. He died in Florence on 15 April 1944, killed by a partisan group for his adherence to the Italian Social Republic. His idealist thought deeply shaped academic culture and the organization of education in Italy.

In this climate, school was no longer merely a place of instruction but became a space of ideological education. The goal was not to form autonomous and aware citizens, but disciplined individuals obedient and loyal to the regime. Alongside school, Fascism built a parallel system of youth organizations that occupied the whole free time of young people, turning education into an all-encompassing project.

The building that now houses the Lussana High School was born within this very vision: not as a school, but as a symbolic and operational place for Fascist youth education.

Why it is a place of memory

Today the Lussana High School is a public school fully aligned with the values of the Italian Constitution. However, the building preserves clear traces of its past, raising important questions about the relationship between space, memory, and history. The architecture, plaques, and remaining symbols are not simple decorative elements, but material documents of an educational project founded on indoctrination, forced discipline, and the suppression of individuality.

“Memory” does not consist only in recalling what happened, but in being able to interpret it critically. Walking through these spaces today means confronting a complex legacy: a place that saw education transformed into an instrument of control and that can now become an opportunity to reflect on the values of freedom, democracy, and civic responsibility. The history of the building invites us to question how education can be used both to emancipate and to subjugate.

In depth: ONB and GIL

The Opera Nazionale Balilla (ONB), established in 1926, and the Gioventù Italiana del Littorio (GIL), which replaced it in 1937, represented the core of the Fascist educational project for youth. All boys and girls aged 6 to 18 were compulsorily enrolled in these organizations, divided by age and gender. Males were guided toward a strongly militarized path, while females were educated according to the traditional model of future mothers and guardians of the family.

Students’ daily life was marked by collective rituals: the Fascist Saturday, with doctrine lessons and gymnastic activities; parades, rallies, public ceremonies; the use of uniforms, symbols, and salutes that reinforced a sense of belonging. Sport was not an end in itself but a tool to forge strong and obedient bodies, ready for military service.

Through ONB and GIL, Fascism also occupied free time: summer camps organized like military camps, Fascist University Groups for older students, the National After-Work organization for workers. In this way, the distinction between school, free time, and private life tended to disappear. The goal was to create an individual totally integrated into the system, for whom personal identity coincided with Fascist identity.

In depth: history of the building

The building on Via Angelo Maj was designed by architect Alziro Bergonzo (1906–1997), an exponent of Rationalism and a central figure of Fascist architecture in Bergamo. Commissioned as the headquarters of the Opera Nazionale Balilla (ONB), it was inaugurated on 22 June 1932. The ONB was the organization charged with the physical and moral education of young people aged 6 to 18, complementing school and closely linked to military preparation.

The monumental and austere structure reflects the regime’s values: order, discipline, strength. On the facade there is still a large plaque dedicated to Sandro Italico Mussolini, the Duce’s nephew and ONB leader, who died very young; in the central part fascist symbols in relief are still visible, material testimonies of a past the building has never completely erased.

Later, the building became the seat of the Gioventù Italiana del Littorio (GIL), which from 1937 replaced the ONB. With the German occupation and the birth of the Italian Social Republic, the building hosted the “avanguardisti moschettieri,” youths aged 15 to 18 enrolled in the National Republican Guard. Despite their young age, these boys were also employed in anti-partisan activities. The basement rooms still preserve writings that testify to the different uses: changing rooms, place of detention and torture, and storage of materials.

In the postwar period, the building changed function and meaning. It first became the seat of the “Paolina Secco Suardo” Teacher Training Institute and, since 1968, of the “Filippo Lussana” Scientific High School. A space born to educate “the Fascist” was thus converted into a place of Republican education.

“Fascism builds a parallel system of youth organizations that occupies the entire free time of young people, turning education into a totalizing project” (E. Gentile, Il culto del littorio, 1993).

Timeline:

  • 1923 - Gentile school reform: the school becomes a central instrument of Fascist education.
  • 1926 - Establishment of the Opera Nazionale Balilla (ONB).
  • 22 June 1932 - Inauguration of the Via Angelo Maj building as ONB headquarters.
  • 1937 - Birth of the Gioventù Italiana del Littorio (GIL), replacing the ONB.
  • 1943-1945 - The building hosts the “avanguardisti moschettieri” of the National Republican Guard.
  • Postwar period - Headquarters of the “Paolina Secco Suardo” Teacher Training Institute.
  • 1968 - The building becomes the seat of the “Filippo Lussana” Scientific High School.
