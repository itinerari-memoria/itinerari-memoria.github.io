Point 13 – Matris Domini Monastery

Historical Itineraries 2.0

Historical Context

We are at the Matris Domini Monastery in Bergamo, at the foot of the Città Alta hill, along the ancient path leading to the historic city. This cloistered place was transformed into a Nazi military prison in the spring of 1944. During the German occupation (1943-1945), the SS requisitioned part of the convent, confining the nuns and setting up cells for prisoners awaiting trial or deportation. Among others, Don Agostino Vismara, a military chaplain unjustly accused of arms trafficking for the Resistance, was imprisoned here. The monastery became a place of detention but also of silent resistance: from nearby houses, such as the Leidi family home, signals of comfort and hope were sent to the prisoners, waving flags to communicate news. This place symbolizes the dual nature of the occupation: the violence of Nazi-Fascist arbitrariness and the tenacious network of civil and religious solidarity that tried to oppose it.

Why it is a Place of Memory

The Matris Domini Monastery is a place of memory because it embodies the forced transformation of a space of spirituality into one of repression, while maintaining a subtle human resistance. Remembering this place means honoring not only the suffering of the detainees but also the courage of a community that, even under occupation, found ways to support the prisoners with extraordinary gestures of solidarity, such as signals from the windows. The story of Don Agostino Vismara, suspended between the hope of a trial never held and the constant threat of deportation, speaks to us of the arbitrariness of power and the vulnerability of rights. Today, this site reminds us how, even in the darkest moments, human dignity can be defended with silent support and community cohesion, fundamental values for a civil and democratic society.

"Don Agostino Vismara still recalls, moved even today, that from the terrace of the Leidi house... they drew reasons for good news."* – This gesture of passive resistance, a signal of hope from above, summarizes the spirit of resilience that permeated those walls.
Don Agostino Vismara, from Don Agostino Vismara: "we have suffered" (Barbara Curtarelli 2021)

Multimedia Content

Don Agostino Vismara at Matris Domini

In May 1944, Don Agostino Vismara was transferred to the German prison of Matris Domini in Bergamo, where he was detained under German control awaiting a trial that would never take place. His imprisonment occurred in a context of suspended legality and fierce political repression.
The documentation preserves traces of censored correspondence, translated into German by the authorities, and letters smuggled out of the prison, signs of attempts to maintain contact with the outside. There are also self-defense notes, written during the wait for the trial.
After Matris Domini, Vismara was transferred to San Vittore and subsequently deported to the Mauthausen and Dachau concentration camps. The Matris Domini prison thus represents a decisive stage in the journey from detention to deportation.
